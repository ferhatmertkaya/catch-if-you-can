# Development labs

Status: reference. Applies to `Assets/CatchIfYouCan/Scenes/Development/**` and
`Assets/CatchIfYouCan/Scripts/Development/**`.

Nine scenes, one per system, each of which is the shipping system with the
game taken away.

---

## 1. Why they exist

Before these, the only way to look at a piece of this game was to load an
investigation: a generated house, a mission, a ghost, a HUD, weather, audio
zones and a player, all at once. Every question — "is the torch in his hand",
"does the crouch actually lower the capsule", "did that shader survive the
build" — was asked in a room with fifty other things in it, and every answer
could be explained away by one of them.

A lab removes the explanations. If the torch is wrong in the equipment lab, it
is the torch.

---

## 2. The two rules

**A lab never owns a persistent service.** It calls the same
`CiycServices.EnsureCore()` every production scene calls, and spawns the
player through the same `PlayerSpawner`. A system that behaves one way in the
lab and another in the game must not be explainable by the lab having built
its own manager, or its own simplified player.

**A lab builds its fixtures in code, not in the scene.** `BuildFixtures()`
runs on entry and constructs everything. The consequence is that the scene
asset is nearly empty, which means:

- a lab costs nothing to merge, and two agents can change two labs freely;
- a lab cannot rot into a second source of truth for room geometry;
- a lab is regenerated by a menu item rather than repaired by hand;
- nobody has to hand-write scene YAML, which is how references get silently
  broken.

## 3. The labs

| Scene | Lab | What is in it |
|---|---|---|
| `DEV_EquipmentLab` | Equipment | A plinth per catalogue entry in catalogue order, labelled with its id and whether it is real or `DEV_PLACEHOLDER`; a drop zone; a wall to throw things at; the starter loadout. |
| `DEV_CharacterLab` | Character | A rail at the player's exact 1.68 m eye height, a 1.3 m crouch gate, a strafe lane, a 1.86 m human reference and a 1 m grid. |
| `DEV_InteractionLab` | Interaction | One of every `IInteractable` in a row 2.6 m apart — door, drawer, light switch, breaker box, hide spot, pickup — each labelled. |
| `DEV_GhostLab` | Ghost | A fixed four-room house with doorways and a spawn marker per room, and a ghost built by the real `GhostFactory`. |
| `DEV_AudioLab` | Audio | Two rooms sharing a doorway, a glass pane, and three silent 3D looping emitters. |
| `DEV_LightingLab` | Lighting | An albedo ladder from 3% to 95%, a cluster of overlapping point lights, and a shader board with one quad per project shader. |
| `DEV_EnvironmentLab` | Environment | A size ladder from 0.25 m to 3 m, an axis gnomon, a 1 m grid, and every prop under `Resources/Props` at authored scale. |
| `DEV_UIInputLab` | UI / Input | The shipping HUD, heading posts at the four compass points, a 20 degree ramp and 0.18 m steps. |
| `DEV_NetworkLab` | Network | A fixed ring of spawn pads and a notice board reading NETWORKING NOT INSTALLED. |

## 4. Notes on individual labs

**Equipment.** The bench is in catalogue order, which is also the order a
compact network encoding would index into, so a reordering shows up here.
Only the flashlight is implemented; the other ten stand next to it as
labelled placeholders, which is the point. The runtime factory used to give
every unimplemented id a working torch, and an unimplemented item that
quietly works is one nobody ever finishes.

**Character.** The eye rail is at 1.68 m and the human reference is 1.86 m
because those are `PlayerFactory`'s own literals. If either number changes,
the lab's constants have to change with it — and that is deliberate: it makes
the duplication visible instead of letting the camera drift.

**Ghost.** The house is hand-built and fixed, not generated. The generator
produces a different house per seed, and a ghost behaving oddly in a house
nobody has seen before is two bugs at once.

**Lighting.** The shader board is the reason this lab exists. A shader that no
material references is stripped from a player build, and the failure normally
surfaces as a magenta surface on a device weeks later. Here it is a missing
quad and a console line, in the editor, today.

**Network.** It builds a room and says so. A lab that looks finished and does
nothing is worse than no lab: someone opens it, sees four spawn pads, and
concludes networking is partly working. No netcode, Relay, Lobby or
Authentication package is installed.

## 5. Creating and opening them

The scenes are created by **Catch If You Can > Development > Create Missing
Lab Scenes**. Because the fixtures are code, a lab scene contains only its
installer, so "Rebuild All Lab Scenes" is safe and cheap.

Each lab has three switches on its installer:

- `buildFixtures` — off gives an empty room, which is what you want while
  chasing something the fixtures themselves affect.
- `spawnPlayer` — off leaves the bootstrap camera looking at the room, which
  is what you want for a screenshot.
- `logLabState` — on by default. A lab that says nothing about its own state
  is a lab you cannot trust a measurement from.

---

## 6. They never ship

`DEV_` scenes must never be enabled in the build list. This is enforced, not
trusted:

- `Scripts/check_dev_scenes.sh` fails CI if a development scene is enabled in
  the build list, or if the production scene identity contains one.
- `CatchIfYouCanBuildMenu` rejects a build whose scene list includes one, and
  filters them out of the enabled scene paths.
- `DevelopmentScenes.IsDevelopmentScenePath` checks both the folder and the
  `DEV_` filename prefix, so a lab moved out of the folder is still caught.

---

## 7. Adding a lab

1. Add a value to `DevelopmentLab`.
2. Add an installer under `Scripts/Development/Labs` deriving from
   `DevelopmentLabInstaller`: implement `Lab`, `BuildFixtures()` and
   `DescribeState()`.
3. Run **Create Missing Lab Scenes**.

Build fixtures from the helpers on the base — floors, walls, room shells with
real doorways, metre grids, the 1.86 m human reference, labels and plinths —
rather than from the production room. A lab that shares the production room
starts answering questions about the production room.
